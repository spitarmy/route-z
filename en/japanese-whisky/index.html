The Allure of Japanese Whisky in Kyoto

Japan's whisky making began right here in the Kyoto prefecture area when the Yamazaki Distillery was established in 1923 at the confluence of the Katsura, Uji, and Kizu rivers. Renowned for pure soft water, distinct four seasons, and aging in rare Japanese Mizunara oak, Japanese whiskies offer extraordinary delicacy, balance, and floral depth.

However, due to worldwide acclaim and strict shortages, many bars either do not stock them or charge exorbitant, hidden prices to tourists. At Bar Route Z, every pour is clearly priced, served with handcrafted crystal ice in fine glassware.

How to Order Japanese Whisky in Japan

In Japanese bar culture, the serving style is considered an art form. You can enjoy your glass in any of the following traditional styles:

  • Straight / Neat (ストレート - Sutoreeto): Served at room temperature in a snifter to appreciate pure aromas and finish. Accompanied by a glass of chilled water (Chaser).
  • On the Rocks (ロック - Rokku): Poured over pure, hand-cut crystal-clear ice. As the ice slowly melts, new layers of fragrance and sweetness unfold.
  • Mizuwari (水割り - Mizuwari): A uniquely Japanese style. Whisky mixed with mineral water (typically 1:2 or 1:2.5 ratio) and stirred vigorously with ice until frosty cold. Softens alcohol intensity while opening subtle flavors.
  • Highball (ハイボール - Haibooru): Whisky mixed with sparkling soda water and chilled with premium ice. Extremely popular across Japan—crisp, refreshing, and clean.
